# Step 1: Extract facts about the current situation

| Ref | Evidence from Scenario | What the evidence tells |
|---|---|---|
| S1 L1 | Kerry Community Digital Archive receives photographs, documents, audio and video contributed by individuals and local organisations | The service handles serval media types from two kinds of contributors (Individuals & local organisations), so the service has to handle various files and sources. |
| S1 L2 | Submission files, descriptions, ownership statements, permissions, review notes and publication decisions are stored across forms, folders and spreadsheets. | Every part of a request is stored in different places. Establishing the full state of a request therefore means checking several locations |
| S2 L1 | A contribution may contain one item or a related collection | Contributions vary from having one item or many items, and change to one can affect the reset of the collection |
| S2 L2 | The contributor may own the material, act for another rights holder or be uncertain about ownership and permissions. | Ownership and authority of material submitted is not always certain |
| S2 L3 | Requests arrive through several channels and may be incomplete. | There is no single-entry point for requests and submissions may be incomplete. Staff must chase missing information rather than receive complete requests |
| S2 L5 | Priorities and exceptions are not applied consistently. | Decisions about which requests come first depends on who handles them. Requests in the same situation may receive different outcomes, which causes fairness and audit risks. |
| S2 L6 | Another person or organisation may assist with a request. | Third parties can be involved with a request |
| S2 L7 | Staff are uncertain what authority is required before detailed information or decisions can be shared. | There's uncertainty around staff in relation to authority over requests which introduces risks and delays |
| S3 L1 | Review considers file quality, description, sensitivity, ownership evidence, access restrictions and relevance. | Reviews are considered on multiple factors, so a request can fail on any one of the several factors |
| S3 L2 | An item can be preserved without being approved for public access. | Keeping an item and publishing it are separate outcomes. The system must be able to hold the material that is not yet cleared for release |
| S3 L3 | Capacity depends on a combination of people, places, resources and timing. | Capacity depends on several factors, and available capacity is not a single number. A place can look free but still may be unusable because of staffing, recourses or timing |
| S3 L4 | A visible vacancy does not necessarily mean that every requirement is satisfied | A vacancy visible on screen cannot be used as a basis for allocation. The requirements must be checked separately |
| S3 L5 | Provisional allocations sometimes remain in place while essential information is still missing. | Places can be held for requests that are not ready, so capacity is tied up without progress. The frequency is also not stated, so the scale of this is unknown |
| S3 L6 | Changes can affect several linked arrangements. | One change can have knock on effects. Without any visible links between arrangements, a change may leave conflicting records behind |
| S4 L1 | Staff record progress, decisions and outstanding actions using notes, messages and spreadsheets. | Progress is recorded informally, and there is no authoritative record of where a request stands, which makes reporting unreliable |
| S4 L2 | . Different teams use status terms differently, making it difficult to identify what is awaiting action and who is responsible. | Status terms are not standardized across teams. Accountability is also lost in this gap |
| S4 L3 | A planned activity may be changed, partially completed, paused or withdrawn | Planned activities can have different states. Each state needs to be recorded accurately, so the record shows what happened |
| S4 L5 | Reviews may generate new actions, revised arrangements or escalation. | A review does not necessarily mean an end of a request. It can reopen the work, change earlier arrangements, or pass that matter to someone else |
| S5 L4 | Connectivity may be unreliable during off-site work. | Staff may need to carry out some work off-site, where they may not have a reliable connection |
| S6 L3 | Administrator: "Provisional arrangements can block capacity long after the original request has stalled." | Some allocations are being held without them being released or confirmed. This leads to the amount of places available for students being decreased |
